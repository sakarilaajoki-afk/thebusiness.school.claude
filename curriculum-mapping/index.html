Student layer

A plain, in-context instruction, “Set your price”, “Agree the B2B deal”. No specification codes, no awarding-body names, no distraction from the task. Each student receives a private report of their recorded decisions and question results.

Teacher layer

The class report lists each final question with its topic, and how many students answered it correctly. The activity rows below link the simulation's decisions to Pearson Edexcel GCSE Business (1BS0), A Level Business (9BS0) and BTEC National Extended Certificate in Business (601/7159/5) topics. These are topic links for teaching and discussion. This page is not a mapping to any exam board and not a judgement against your exam board's assessment criteria.

What the reports contain

At the end of the lesson each student downloads a personal report of their recorded decisions, question results and reflection. The teacher downloads the class report: each firm's result, each student's recorded actions and questions, and each final question with its topic and how many students answered it correctly. These records support follow-up discussion. They are indicative, not official or predicted exam results, and simulation records do not count as BTEC qualification assessment evidence.

How to read it

This guide to topics was prepared by TBS Education with reference to the published Pearson specifications (GCSE 1BS0 Issue 2; A Level 9BS0 Issue 1; BTEC 601/7159/5), checked on 8 November 2026. It is not a mapping to any exam board, and it is not endorsed, approved or accredited by Pearson. A recorded action shows that a decision was taken in the simulation, not that the topic is understood: use it as a prompt for questioning, not as a judgement. Team chat is never treated as curriculum evidence.

Your qualification and level

Before the lesson, choose your class. The list has 14 exam board courses and 7 plain classes, such as GCSE Business. Your choice sets the level (Level 2, GCSE-level, or Level 3, A level-level) and the topics on offer, and names your class in the reports.

The final questions are written for the level, not for one specification. Each question has its own topic, shown on the class report. The questions are not endorsed by any awarding body.
