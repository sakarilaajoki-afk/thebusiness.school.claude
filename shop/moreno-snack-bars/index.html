What is inside

One original, fictional case for three 55-minute lessons plus homework (about 15 minutes after Lesson 1 and 12 after Lesson 3). A family snack-bar business in a growing coastal city must choose: a cheap lunchbox bar for a supermarket chain, or a premium sports bar sold online and through gyms.

Learners analyse survey data and market share and plan a marketing mix; compare labour productivity, costs, break-even and margin of safety, and complete a break-even chart; then weigh finance, recruitment, pay methods, interest rates and an exchange-rate cost rise before writing a justified recommendation of about 150 words. Data come in appendices (survey table, market report and chart, news items), as in a case-study paper.

  1. Lesson1

    Which market should the new bar target?

    Key-terms starter, research methods and sampling, the survey appendix and percentage analysis, a market report with bar chart, market share, mass and niche, a four-Ps marketing mix.

  2. Lesson2

    Making the bars: production, costs and break-even

    Job, batch and flow, labour productivity, a cost table, contribution, break-even and margin of safety, a break-even chart to complete and a price-cut test.

  3. Lesson3

    Finance, people and the final decision

    Internal and external finance, finance needed and source choice, promoting Amir, salary against piece-rate pay, a news appendix with a cost-rise recalculation, a planning page and a 150-word recommendation.
