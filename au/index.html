Our story

Built from a real lesson no classroom ever taught

That one mistake led to €8,400 in debt recovery. No teacher, no textbook and no careers advisor had ever mentioned it could happen.

A growing share of students already run a side hustle by senior year. Social media has made entrepreneurship feel easy, but nobody teaches the financial fundamentals that make or break a young business.

What if students could make those mistakes safely, before they cost real money? That question became The Business School.

“At 17, I organised large-scale events, took real financial risks, and learned the hard way what happens when you miss a tax return.”

Founder, TBS Education Ltd Oy · Lahti, Finland

Proudly built in Finland, where pedagogy is founded on trust, equality and learning by doing.

Before this game, I ran a shop and a live event in Lahti

I organised a live music event and ran a clothing shop, Suitman, with a business partner. The local paper wrote about both. The shop closed in January 2026. Selling branded clothing in that city was not very profitable. I learned that by running it, not from a lesson. That is why students in The Business School run a firm and make the decisions themselves.

The simulation

Real business decisions, not a quiz

Firms of three or four students compete in one shared market, live. We moved the simulation from a coffee shop chain to a vehicle dealership network, because a missing product often means a real negotiation with a competitor, not just a lower price.

How to start

From a free trial to your first lesson

1

Get ready

Ask for a free trial and we email you a teacher login. Open the game, choose the course, the topic and the lesson length, from 30 to 75 minutes. You get a 6-digit PIN.

2

Students join

Students open play.thebusiness.school on a laptop, desktop or Chromebook and type the PIN and a first name. Press Start: with 6 or more the game makes firms of 3 or 4 and gives everyone a role; with 1 to 5 each student runs their own firm.

3

Run the lesson

Three rounds run on the clock. Each student’s Do now card shows their next step. Pause to discuss, and start an optional class event when it suits you.

4

After the lesson

Each student downloads their own report. You download one class pack: the class report and every student’s report.

Teacher tutorial: how to start a lesson, in 85 seconds.

After every session

Every student gets a personal report

When the simulation ends, each student downloads a private report of what they did, not a generic score. It is indicative, not an exam result, and it ends with a certificate of completion that records participation only. Assessment stays with the teacher.

Technical details

Built for the classroom, no IT department required

Browser-based

Questions

What teachers ask first

Is it free for teachers?＋

The teacher demo is free and needs no credit card. Licensing is per school or district, per year, with a free classroom trial before any commitment.

Which levels does it fit?＋

Business in Years 9 to 12: a Years 9–10 level and a Years 11–12 level. When you set up a lesson you choose the course and the topic; the six questions at the end come from that topic.

Do students need to install anything or make accounts?＋

No. It runs in the browser on a laptop, desktop or Chromebook, one screen per student. Students open the link and join with a 6-digit PIN and a first name, nothing to download, no logins to manage.

How many students, and how long does it take?＋

Up to 40 students. Most classes play in teams of 3 or 4, and smaller classes run a firm each. After up to 3 minutes of firm setup there are three rounds, and you set each round to 10, 15, 20 or 25 minutes. The rounds move on by themselves when the clock runs out, so it never stalls.

What does the teacher see and control?＋

A live control panel: pause and resume the rounds, start an optional class event, see every student’s role and activity, and project the live chart of every firm’s net position. Afterwards each student downloads a private report, and you download the class report. The game checks only the multiple-choice Knowledge Check answers. Nothing is turned into a mark, and assessment stays with the teacher.
